Act I

Why

Copenhagen needs new water. But the problem is social before it is technical.

0123456789012345678901 / 11 · The problem

Copenhagen drinks from one aquifer, and the gap is growing.

Before talking about solutions, the facts: one underground source, a projected shortfall, and a future that will keep changing.

1.1

One source, a growing gap

Copenhagen's real vulnerability, identified when the team took on Ramboll's challenge around digital technologies for resilient water systems, is its dependence on a single underground aquifer. Projections point to a shortfall of about 48,000 m³/day toward 2040. This project does not claim to close that gap: for now, there is no way to do so.

The obvious reading is "we will run out, so we need another source". That reading leads straight to a catalog of alternatives and a bet on which one is right. It also forgets the people who will drink the water.

Projected shortfall · toward 2040−48,000 m³/day
Single aquiferGap

≈ 17.5 million m³ per year. HydroCelium adds one accepted source at a time; none is expected to close this alone.

1.2

Why the obvious answer is not enough

Climate, demand, regulation and technology will all keep moving. A supply that can only be swapped for another fixed supply stays exposed; one that learns what people accept, and where, does not.

And there is a second problem hidden behind the first one. It is not in the aquifer. It is in the people who will drink the water.

So we bring Copenhagen

An intelligent and adaptive growth system.

Intelligent

It reads water data and people's reactions together, and recommends; the operator decides.

Adaptive

It changes direction when people or conditions change, instead of locking the city into one bet.

Growth system

It grows like a mycelium: explore cheaply, listen at the tips, invest where it found acceptance.

Its name is HydroCelium. To see why it has to work this way, look first at the people, and at how we test ideas.

0123456789012345678902 / 11 · Not only a water-source problem

Copenhageners are proud of their water. People matter as much as the source.

A new source is not only an engineering decision. If people don't trust it, they won't drink it, and the investment fails. That makes this a social problem before it is a technical one.

We do not invest in prototypes. We invest in

Pretotyping

/ˈpriː.tə.taɪ.pɪŋ/ · noun

Testing whether people want something before you build it, with the smallest possible investment.

Term coined by Alberto Savoia at Google. HydroCelium applies it to water: fake the experience, measure the reaction, then decide.

PretotypeIs this the right idea?
  • Weeks, not years
  • A fraction of the cost
  • Measures acceptance and trust
  • Allowed to fail cheaply
PrototypeCan we build the idea right?
  • Months to years
  • Real infrastructure and permits
  • Measures yield and viability
  • Only for ideas people accepted

We test acceptance, perception and trust. Safety is decided by sanitary and technical controls, and an idea that fails them never reaches a Hy Tap.

Act II

What

We learned from mycelium how to grow without betting on one direction.

0123456789012345678904 / 11 · Inspired by mycelium

What the fungus does, and how we answer the same way.

A mycelium has no centre and no plan. It grows, tests, reinforces and abandons, and still finds water. We copied its mechanisms, not just its shape: every decision in HydroCelium is one the fungus already makes.

4.1

What a mycelium does

Mycelium is the underground body of a fungus: a web of thin threads called hyphae that can cover a whole forest floor. It has no brain and no map. It learns about its ground only through the very ends of its threads, the hyphal tips.

Each tip senses moisture and nutrients. Where it finds them, the fungus sends more material and the thread thickens. Where it finds nothing, the thread is withdrawn and reused. Over time, the network ends up exactly where the resources are.

That is the problem Copenhagen has with new water: many possible directions, no certainty about which one people will accept, and a limited budget. The fungus solves it every day by exploring cheaply, listening at its tips and investing only where it found something.

So HydroCelium does the same. Its tips are the Hy Taps. What they sense is acceptance. And its decisions are the fungus's decisions.

0123456789012345678906 / 11 · The solution

HydroCelium: an intelligent and adaptive growth system, inspired by mycelium.

HydroCelium finds the meeting points between people's acceptance and trust and the future ideas for new water sources, to show which direction the city should take.

6.1

What it is, precisely

HydroCelium is a digital system that represents the city's water ideas as a living network, the way a mycelium represents a forest floor. Its nodes are the Hy Taps where people react and the pretotypes of new sources. Its links grow or fade with acceptance.

It integrates water availability, demand, temporal variability, storage capacity and system constraints, and compares possible configurations before any decision touches the physical system.

It sits on top of the current aqueduct rather than replacing it. It does not replace the operator either: it generates scenarios, evaluates alternatives and presents data-backed recommendations, so the people responsible for the system can decide with more information, never autonomously.

We do not propose a prototype, and we do not tell the city which new source it needs. We propose pretotyping: finding out what people accept before investing.

Design principle

Adaptability should not mean handing control over to an automated system; it should mean giving the people responsible for the system better information and more options when conditions change.

What the operator sees is a transparent view: the current conditions, what is expected to change, which options are feasible, how people responded to each idea, and what trade-offs each option involves.

In the mycelium

The fungus is not one thread but a whole body: tips, cords and junctions working as one network.

In HydroCelium

    7.4

    What this is not

    Citizen opinion is not a potability test.

    Reactions measure acceptance, perception, trust and experience. Whether water is safe to drink is decided by sanitary and technical controls, and an idea that fails them never reaches a Hy Tap, however well it might taste.

    0123456789012345678908 / 11 · The topology

    A map of connections, not distances.

    Our real contribution is a water-supply topology: a field of AI that finds the big patterns in complex data, what connects and where the holes are. Copenhagen invests only where people's acceptance and technical data meet.

    8.1

    From data points to one surface

    Every Hy Tap and every source idea is a point full of data. Layer on layer, technical data, people's perception and the Hy Tap network are stacked over the city and merged into one surface. Its peaks are the best matches between feasibility and acceptance.

    8.2

    White water and blue water

    In Copenhagen only 10% of the water the city uses is for drinking. Every source has to meet the same quality standards; the Hy Taps then add perception. The most feasible and most accepted sources become white water for drinking. Sources that meet every standard but are less accepted become blue water for everything else: industry, laundry and toilets, irrigation, street cleaning. Nothing that meets the standards is wasted.

    8.3

    Clean water, matched to demand

    Clean water is a scale, from drinkable white to industrial blue. On grid, each new source takes its place on that scale and is joined to the existing pipes that lead to the buildings needing that quality of water. Off grid, the same logic works at building scale: rainwater collected on the roofs fills an underground tank that feeds a street tap for washing.

    8.4

    SON: a System Off-grid Network

    Some new sources meet every standard but people will not drink them. Instead of pushing them into the city grid, a SON stores the water in a shared tank and runs one short pipe to the shops and workshops nearby, for toilets, cleaning and car washing. Homes keep their drinking water from the city grid. Extending the grid costs millions and takes decades; a SON costs thousands and takes years.

    Green roofs

    Vegetation and substrate on existing rooftops retain and filter rainwater; the surplus is collected in shared tanks by block.

    Draft volume estimate48,000 m³/day[VERIFICAR]
    Pretotype not startedAcceptance: measured at Hy Taps

    Main limitation: Most of the water is retained and evapotranspired by the vegetation itself; available roof area sets the performance ceiling.

    02 / 08 · Pretotype

    Aquifer recharge (ASR)

    Treated water is injected underground during low-demand periods and extracted when the city needs it most: the aquifer itself acts as a seasonal tank.

    Draft volume estimate80,000 m³/day[VERIFICAR]
    Pretotype not startedAcceptance: measured at Hy Taps

    Main limitation: Requires carefully managed injection and extraction cycles and environmental permits over the aquifer itself.

    03 / 08 · Pretotype

    Permeable pavement

    Squares and streets with porous pavement let rain infiltrate; a fraction is captured before reaching the sewer and sent to the reserve network.

    Draft volume estimate60,000 m³/day[VERIFICAR]
    Pretotype not startedAcceptance: measured at Hy Taps

    Main limitation: Depends directly on rainfall, and fine pores clog over time without regular maintenance.

    04 / 08 · Pretotype

    HVAC condensate

    Water already condensed by existing buildings' air-conditioning units, today discarded, is redirected and added to the network instead of lost down the drain.

    Draft volume estimate40,000 m³/day[VERIFICAR]
    Pretotype not startedAcceptance: measured at Hy Taps

    Main limitation: Only exists where cooling is already active, so its volume follows cooling demand, not water demand.

    05 / 08 · Pretotype

    Atmospheric water generators (AWG)

    Compact units cool humid air until it condenses into clean water, powered by their own solar and wind energy, with a minimal land footprint.

    Draft volume estimate36,000 m³/day[VERIFICAR]
    Pretotype not startedAcceptance: measured at Hy Taps

    Main limitation: The most energy-intensive of the seven systems, and its output drops under low ambient humidity.

    06 / 08 · Pretotype

    Hygroscopic surfaces

    Passive façade panels capture ambient moisture at night and release it as clean water by day, using only solar heat, with no electricity or pumps.

    Draft volume estimate32,000 m³/day[VERIFICAR]
    Pretotype not startedAcceptance: measured at Hy Taps

    Main limitation: With no active parts to boost output, it needs a large façade area for a moderate volume.

    07 / 08 · Pretotype

    Fog harvesting

    Vertical meshes on the Øresund coastline intercept sea fog; droplets coalesce, fall by gravity and are stored for non-potable use or further treatment.

    Draft volume estimate24,000 m³/day[VERIFICAR]
    Pretotype not startedAcceptance: measured at Hy Taps

    Main limitation: Only works where fog is frequent, an opportunistic coastal source, not a constant one.

    10.2

    The path every idea follows

    Whatever the idea, the route is the same. That sameness lets HydroCelium compare very different ideas: rain on a roof, moisture in the air and fog on the coast all end up as the same kind of record, read the same way.

    10.3

    What a viability study measures

    Once people have accepted an idea, a viability pilot runs for roughly 6 to 12 months in a single zone of Copenhagen (years 3 to 5), long enough to see how the source behaves across conditions rather than on one good day.

    • M01Quantity

      How much water, in total.

    • M02Frequency

      How often it arrives.

    • M03Quality

      What the water contains.

    • M04Treatment needs

      What it takes to make it fit to drink.

    • M05Costs

      Build, run and maintain.

    • M06Weather behavior

      How it reacts to rain, heat and humidity.

    • M07Storage

      How and where it waits.

    • M08Efficiency

      What comes out against what goes in.

    10.4

    An illustrative viability pilot

    Lengths differ by source; this is only the shape of a typical viability pilot, drawn over twelve months. The citizen test begins once measurements show the water can be treated safely, never before.

    A pretotype is allowed to fail. One that ends in "change direction" has done its job: it kept the city from investing in something people would not accept.
